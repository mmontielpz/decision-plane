# Decision Signals — Decision Plane

## Purpose

This document defines what **decision signals** are within **Decision Plane**, how they are generated, how they are persisted, and how they are allowed to be consumed.

Decision signals exist to **support human inspection and downstream reasoning**, not to drive execution.

They are **informational artifacts**, not decisions.

---

## Signal Philosophy

Decision Plane treats signals as:

* **Descriptive**, not prescriptive
* **Explicit**, not inferred
* **Persisted**, not transient
* **Contextual**, not authoritative
* **Explainable**, not opaque

A signal answers:

> “What property or uncertainty exists in this document or prediction?”

A signal never answers:

> “What should be done?”

---

## Definition of a Decision Signal

A **decision signal** is a persisted indicator derived from:

* processing outputs
* prediction outputs
* structural or data quality checks
* analytical evaluation

Signals describe **conditions**, **uncertainties**, or **anomalies** that may be relevant to human or external decision-making.

---

## What Signals Are Not

Decision signals are explicitly **not**:

* decisions
* actions
* recommendations
* policies
* thresholds tied to enforcement
* workflow instructions

Signals do **not** imply correctness, priority, or outcome.

---

## Signal Generation Sources

Signals may be generated by:

### 1. Processing Signals

Derived during processing execution, including:

* schema mismatches
* parsing failures
* missing required fields
* structural anomalies
* data quality flags

These signals explain **why processing behaved as it did**.

---

### 2. Prediction Signals

Derived from model inference outputs, including:

* low confidence indicators
* high uncertainty regions
* missing or invalid predictions
* out-of-distribution indicators

These signals explain **limitations or uncertainty in model outputs**, not their correctness.

---

### 3. Analytical Signals

Derived post-hoc from historical data, including:

* drift indicators
* stability warnings
* degradation patterns

These signals explain **system behavior over time**.

---

## Signal Persistence Model

All signals are persisted in the `document_signals` entity.

Each signal record must include:

* document identifier
* signal type (enumerated, versioned)
* signal value (typed, explicit)
* optional confidence
* source reference (processing step, prediction run, analysis job)
* timestamp

Signals are:

* append-only
* immutable once written
* historically traceable

Signals are **never overwritten or resolved**.

---

## Signal Semantics

### Informational Only

Signals must be interpretable **without embedded intent**.

Allowed examples:

* `low_model_confidence`
* `missing_required_field`
* `ocr_quality_degraded`
* `prediction_unavailable`
* `distribution_shift_detected`

Disallowed examples:

* `auto_reject`
* `requires_approval`
* `send_to_review_now`
* `block_processing`

---

### No Threshold Semantics

Signals must **not** encode:

* decision thresholds
* business cutoffs
* acceptance or rejection logic

If a threshold exists, it must be:

* external
* explicit
* configurable outside Decision Plane
* applied by downstream systems or humans

---

## Signal Versioning

Signals types are versioned explicitly.

Rules:

* New signal types may be added
* Existing signal semantics may not change
* Deprecated signals must remain interpretable historically

Signal evolution is **additive only**.

---

## Signal Consumption Rules

Consumers of decision signals must assume:

* signals are incomplete
* signals may be noisy
* signals may conflict
* signals may arrive late

Decision Plane does **not**:

* rank signals
* prioritize signals
* aggregate signals into decisions
* suppress signals automatically

Interpretation belongs outside the system.

---

## Relationship to Read Models

Inspection views (queues, dashboards, summaries):

* may display signals
* may group or filter signals
* may annotate signals visually

They may **not**:

* resolve signals
* mutate signal state
* infer decisions
* hide conflicting signals

Read models are **presentation layers**, not reasoning engines.

---

## Signal and Replay Interaction

During replay:

* new signals may be generated
* prior signals remain intact
* signal comparisons are allowed
* no signal is retroactively modified

Replay surfaces **differences**, not corrections.

---

## Prohibited Signal Behaviors

The following are explicitly forbidden:

* signal-triggered actions
* auto-resolution of signals
* policy execution based on signals
* suppression of signals due to downstream outcomes
* mutation of historical signals

Any such behavior violates system guarantees.

---

## Auditability Requirements

For audit purposes:

* every signal must be traceable to a source
* signal generation must be reproducible
* absence of a signal must be explainable
* signal history must be inspectable per document

Signals are **first-class audit artifacts**.

---

## Evolution Rules

Allowed:

* New signal types
* New signal sources
* Additional metadata
* New inspection views

Disallowed:

* Embedding policy semantics
* Introducing implicit prioritization
* Using signals to drive execution

---

## Final Statement

Decision signals exist to **make uncertainty explicit**.

If a signal begins to:

* imply an action
* encode a decision
* suppress human judgment
* or automate behavior

then it is no longer a signal, and it does not belong in Decision Plane.
